mm-bot.js — market maker

What the bot actually does

A 250ms loop that reads Binance spot to price two Polymarket outcomes (Up / Down) on its own model, quotes passive bids below that price, and cuts fast when the model turns. No resting offers, no requirement that the pair sum under $1 — modeled on wallet 0xeebde7a0….

The mechanism, piece by piece

Pricing

One formula prices both legs: Φ(ln(spot/strike) / (σ√τ)). Down is just 1 − Up. σ is realized vol over the trailing 10 minutes of Binance ticks, re-cached every second.

Quoting

Passive bids only, on both legs independently — no requirement that Up + Down price sums under $1. Bid = fair − MM_MARGIN, never crossing the ask.

Risk gates

A leg is blocked from quoting if: outside the quote window, at the inventory or capital cap, or already priced (by model or book) below MM_EXIT_FAIR_FLOOR + MM_EXIT_QUOTE_BUFFER.

Exit

A held position is cut on any of: book/fair under the floor, fair dropped MM_EXIT_FAIR_DROP from entry, or held past MM_EXIT_MAX_HOLD_SEC while still under 0.50.

Hold

Anything not cut rides to resolution. closeWindow() moves it into a settling pool that polls gamma-api for the outcome and journals a win or loss — this is where most of the modeled edge is supposed to come from.

Observability

Every cut and resolution is journaled; a live snapshot and a fair-vs-market trail are written roughly once a second and served by a separate dashboard process on its own port.

Open issue — quote cooldown after a cut

When a cut finishes, leg.exited and leg.position both reset immediately, so the very next tick can re-quote the exact leg just sold — nothing remembers that this leg was just decided against.

Measured this session: 22 of 27 live cuts were repeat buy/cut cycles on the same leg within one window, accounting for −$13.26 of the total −$17.46 cut P&L. One leg was bought and cut five times in a single 5-minute window.
